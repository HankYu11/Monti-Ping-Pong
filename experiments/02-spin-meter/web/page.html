      Revolutions per minute: matched hand counts on 5 of 5 test serves (within about 5%).

      Backspin or topspin: correct on 4 of 4 labelled serves so far.

      Off the paddle, after the bounce: when the bounce is in view, both are measured. Off the paddle is the serve's own spin; after the bounce is what the receiver faces, usually a little less.

      Sidespin amount: experimental. On a dim, small ball the axis can be off by tens of degrees. Serves labelled by your coach will show how far.

      What to work on

        How it works: from the spin, the ball's speed off the paddle and the toss's speed into it. Physics of the contact says how thin it was and whether the ball gripped the rubber or slid.

        How sure: on 80 simulated serves run through this meter it answered 55 and was right on 53. On the real serves so far the weak one read as pushed and the strong ones as strong. Needs a side-on view and a backspin serve.

        How to record

        1. Mark the ball: colour one half black along the seam (two coats).
        2. Add three colour dots on the white half, each about 8 mm across (a bit bigger than a pencil's eraser), filled solid: red, blue and green. Put them a finger-width (1 cm) from the seam, evenly spread: like 12, 4 and 8 o'clock when you look at the white half straight on. Permanent markers work; avoid pale, neon or metallic ones. The dots let short or slow-spinning serves be read, and spins the black half alone can't follow.
        3. Slow motion at 240 fps, phone in landscape, held still: on a tripod, or leaned against something solid.
        4. Best: beside the table, level with the server's half, about 1–1.5 m from the path from paddle to first bounce, 40–60 cm above the table and tilted down a little, so the table is behind the ball. Then the spin is read off the paddle and again after the bounce.
        5. Also works: from the far end of the table, a little to one side, looking back at the server. The ball comes toward the camera; usually only the spin after the bounce can be read.
        6. Not from behind the server: the ball flies away, gets small and blurs.
        7. The toss doesn't need to be in view: the spin comes from the paddle.
        8. Bright light: daylight or extra lamps. Dim light blurs the marking.
        9. Several serves in one video is fine: leave a second or two between serves and keep the camera still. Each serve gets its own reading.
