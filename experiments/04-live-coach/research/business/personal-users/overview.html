Personal users: who they are, how we get them

A 90-day plan to win individual players, starting in Taiwan, under NT$50,000. Figures with a source are linked; my own estimates are marked Est.. Nothing has been posted and nobody has been contacted.

Landing page draftzh-TW, price cards, sign-up you can read Launch kit5 video scripts, 3 posts, outreach, 15 creators Files on GitHubbranch claude/biz-personal-users

In short

  • The phone gate is the main problem in Taiwan. iPhone is about 52% of phones in use and Samsung about 19% of sales; neither can run the app. Pixel, the one tested phone, is about 3% (StatCounter, Oct 2026; 鉅亨網, Dec 2025 retail share). Only about 3–15% of Taiwanese players can use the app today Est., depending on how many OPPO, vivo and Xiaomi models pass.
  • So the first 90 days are a test with real buyers, not a mass launch. Collect phone models with every sign-up, let people try the app on our Pixel at clubs and tournaments, and sell pre-marked ball kits. Count iPhone sign-ups to decide when the iOS port starts.
  • First segment: serious adult club players. Second: parents of junior players. Both already pay NT$700–1,200 for a private lesson (Pro360), and a year of the app costs about one lesson.
  • Prices: free (30 serves a day), NT$149 a month or NT$1,190 a year (NT$890 early bird), and a NT$1,290 kit with 24 marked balls and a year of Player. Spinsight's player plan is about NT$2,100 a year plus its own balls.
  • Cheapest, fastest channels: community posts, our own short videos, and the #發球幾轉 serve spin challenge. Next come small Taiwanese creators (3–55K subscribers), who reply and are affordable. English markets follow in weeks 8–13.
  • 90-day target Est.: 800 sign-ups, 180 activated users, about 45 paying subscribers and 30 kits, for about NT$80,000 of revenue from a NT$49,000 budget. The bigger result is a phone list and an iOS waitlist count.

Three decisions for you

1. When does the iOS port start?

Half of Taiwan uses iPhones, and most Android sales are Samsung, which blocks 240 fps for apps. Android-only caps Taiwan at a few percent of players.

My recommendation: run the 90 days on Android, but set a trigger now. If iPhone sign-ups exceed 300, or exceed 40% of all sign-ups, by week 6, start iOS in week 7. Spinsight already measures only on iPhone 13 and newer, so iPhones 8–12 are open.

2. Do we sell pre-marked ball kits now, or wait for the JOOLA Spinball test (E2)?

Marking balls is the second-biggest friction. A first batch of 40 kits costs about NT$13,000 and about 16 hours of marking (960 balls at a minute each) Est..

My recommendation: sell kits now, in a batch of 40, and run E2 in parallel. Kits also solve the payment problem before the Google Play listing, because a physical product with an activation code can be sold through our own shop.

3. Do you approve the prices, and a zh-TW voice before the public Taiwan launch?

Prices: NT$149 a month, NT$1,190 a year, NT$890 early bird for the first 300, a NT$1,290 kit, and 30 free serves a day. The voice is English today. It speaks only short lines, so a zh-TW voice is a small change to the app.

My recommendation: approve the prices as a test that runs until week 13. Ask the app team for a zh-TW voice option before the Play listing in week 7. "幾轉" spoken in Mandarin is the product's best demo.
