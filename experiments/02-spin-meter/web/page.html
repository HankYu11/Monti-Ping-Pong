      Revolutions per minute: matched hand counts on 5 of 5 test serves (within about 5%).

      Backspin or topspin: correct on 4 of 4 labelled serves so far.

      Off the paddle, after the bounce: when the bounce is in view, both are measured. Off the paddle is the serve's own spin; after the bounce is what the receiver faces, usually a little less.

      Sidespin amount: experimental. On a dim, small ball the axis can be off by tens of degrees. Serves labelled by your coach will show how far.

      How to record

      1. Mark the ball: colour one half black along the seam, draw one 5 mm line over the white half, and add one dot beside it.
      2. Slow motion at 240 fps, phone in landscape, held still: on a tripod, or leaned against something solid.
      3. Best: beside the table, level with the server's half, about 1–1.5 m from the path from paddle to first bounce, 40–60 cm above the table and tilted down a little, so the table is behind the ball. Then the spin is read off the paddle and again after the bounce.
      4. Also works: from the far end of the table, a little to one side, looking back at the server. The ball comes toward the camera; usually only the spin after the bounce can be read.
      5. Not from behind the server: the ball flies away, gets small and blurs.
      6. The toss doesn't need to be in view: the spin comes from the paddle.
      7. Bright light: daylight or extra lamps. Dim light blurs the marking.
      8. Several serves in one video is fine: leave a second or two between serves and keep the camera still. Each serve gets its own reading.
