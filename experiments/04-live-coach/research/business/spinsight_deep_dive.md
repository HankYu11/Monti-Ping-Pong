# Spinsight: who pays, and how the company got here

*Research date: 6 November 2026. Facts come from the linked sources. **[Est.]** marks my own reading or estimate. Most of what is public comes from Spinsight itself or from its partners' press releases. No revenue, user numbers or contract values are published anywhere I could find.*

---

## 0. In short

- **Spinsight is not a start-up in the usual sense. It is a unit of ESN**, the German company in Hofheim (Lower Franconia) that makes table tennis rubbers for other brands. ESN has about 290 employees ([ESN](https://www.esn-tt.de/), [career-mainfranken](https://career-mainfranken.com/top-arbeitgeber/esn-technology-group-gmbh/)). The owners, Dr. Georg Nicklas and Jonas Nicklas, sit on its steering committee ([Spinsight company page](https://spinsight.com/company/)). No outside funding round is on record.
- **It took nine years to reach a phone app.** The idea came in 2015 and the lab was built in 2017–18. A camera-based "Elite" system followed in 2020–21. The company was split out in 2022, and the iPhone app came out of beta only in December 2025.
- **Who pays, from the most money to the least [Est.]:**
  1. ESN itself, as the parent.
  2. Federations and performance centres, for the Elite system at about €2,500 a month.
  3. Clubs and coaches, through Team Pro (€25–50 a month) and balls.
  4. Players, through Player Pro (€5–10 a month) and balls.
  5. Brands, which buy data for rubber development (andro).
- **The federation deals look like sponsorship and distribution, not federations buying a product [Est.].** Regional associations get starter kits and pass discount codes to their clubs. The national DTTB deal was arranged by the federation's sponsorship-sales agency.
- **Public money has mostly failed.** The DTTB tried twice to get about €180,000 from the federal sports science institute (BISp) for two years of Spinsight use. Both applications were rejected for lack of money. Only a small university teaching project, paid from state funds, went ahead ([DTTB reports to the 20th Bundestag, 2025](https://www.tischtennis.de/fileadmin/documents/Satzungen_Ordnungen/2026/Stand_BT_2025/2025_Berichte_an_den_20._DTTB-Bundestag.pdf)). This matters for any government proposal of ours; see §4.

### 2.3 What this tells us [Est.]

- **The money so far is a parent's strategic bet, not a market.** ESN can afford many years of losses because Spinsight helps its main business: it shows brands how rubbers perform, and it ties ESN to federations and top players.
- **Federations are Spinsight's sales channel, not its paying customers.** It trades kits, coach training and sponsorship for access to clubs. We can't match that money in Germany. It also means the paying end-user market is still mostly untested, even for them.
- **The Elite system is going away.** The DTTB says the app will replace it in daily training. Spinsight is moving from €2,500-a-month installations to €60–300-a-year apps plus balls, which is the same ground we're on.
- **Their weak points still stand:** measuring needs an iPhone 13 or newer, Android can only view, balls must be theirs, and outside Germany and France there is little traction.

## 4. What Spinsight's story says about a government proposal

The DTTB's own report shows that the obvious route failed. The federation asked a federal sports science fund to pay for two years of *using a company's system* (about €180k). It was turned down twice for lack of money. What did go ahead was smaller and framed as **education research**: a university testing whether the app improves students' technique, paid from state teaching funds.

Lessons for us [Est.]:

1. **Don't ask a government to buy our product for elite athletes.** Ask it to fund a public outcome that a cheap tool makes possible. Candidate framings:
   - **School and university PE:** does instant spoken feedback improve technique? Our app runs offline on a phone and costs almost nothing per school.
   - **Grassroots coaching and coach education**, for example in regional training bases.
   - **Health and inclusion:** table tennis for older people and for Parkinson's is being studied (the same DTTB report mentions a Parkinson's application to a German pension insurer). Spinsight works with the German Disabled Sports Association.
   - **Domestic sports-tech or AI innovation:** a low-cost alternative to imported systems that ships with an open dataset.
2. **Apply with a research partner** (a sports university or a federation's science board), not alone. All of Spinsight's public-funding attempts went through the DTTB and DSHS Cologne.
3. **Keep the first ask small.** A pilot in 5–10 schools or clubs with a clear measurement plan. The DSHS project shows that small, well-framed projects get through when large ones don't.
4. **Our cost is the argument.** One Elite system costs about €30,000 a year. A school phone kit with marked balls and a 3D-printed stencil costs well under €500 [Est.].

**Open question for you:** which government, and which kind of programme do you have in mind? Examples: a sports administration, an education ministry, an innovation or SBIR-style grant, or a city. The right framing and partner depend on it.
